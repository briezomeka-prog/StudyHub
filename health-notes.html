GECH 1101

Health Principles

Expanded study notes covering the meaning of health, dimensions of health, nutrition, hygiene, physical activity, mental health, disease prevention, sanitation, first aid, substance abuse and healthy living.

Introduction to Health Principles

Health is one of the most important requirements for a productive and meaningful life. A healthy person is better able to learn, work, participate in family and community activities and achieve personal goals.

Health is not simply the absence of disease. It involves physical, mental, emotional, social and other aspects of well-being. A person may not have a diagnosed illness but may still have poor health because of inadequate nutrition, stress, lack of sleep, unhealthy relationships or an unsafe environment.

Health Principles therefore studies the knowledge, behaviours and practices that help individuals and communities maintain good health, prevent disease and improve their quality of life.

1. Meaning of Health

Health is a state of well-being that includes physical, mental, emotional and social aspects of life. It is broader than simply being free from disease.

Health can change throughout a person's life. Factors such as age, environment, nutrition, lifestyle, income, education, relationships and access to healthcare can influence health.

Example: A student who eats well, gets enough sleep, exercises regularly, manages stress and maintains positive relationships is supporting several dimensions of health at the same time.

2. Dimensions of Health

Health has several interconnected dimensions. A person's condition in one dimension can affect other dimensions.

Dimension Explanation
Physical health Relates to the functioning and condition of the body, including nutrition, exercise, rest and disease prevention.
Mental health Relates to thinking, learning, decision-making and coping with the normal challenges of life.
Emotional health Involves recognizing, expressing and managing emotions in healthy ways.
Social health Relates to relationships, communication and interaction with other people.
Spiritual health May involve meaning, values, purpose and personal beliefs.

3. Determinants of Health

Determinants of health are the conditions and factors that influence the health of individuals and communities.

Major determinants include:

  • Individual lifestyle and behaviour.
  • Nutrition.
  • Physical environment.
  • Social relationships.
  • Education.
  • Income and employment.
  • Access to healthcare.
  • Housing and sanitation.
  • Genetic and biological factors.

This means that health is influenced by much more than medical treatment. Improving living conditions and healthy behaviours can also contribute greatly to better health.

4. Personal Hygiene

Personal hygiene refers to practices that help keep the body clean and reduce the spread of infection and disease.

Examples of personal hygiene include:

  • Regular bathing.
  • Washing hands with soap and clean water.
  • Brushing teeth regularly.
  • Keeping fingernails clean and trimmed.
  • Wearing clean clothes.
  • Maintaining clean hair and scalp.
  • Practicing appropriate menstrual hygiene.

Good hygiene protects both the individual and other people because many infections can spread through contaminated hands, surfaces, food and bodily fluids.

5. Hand Hygiene

Hand hygiene is one of the simplest and most effective ways to reduce the spread of many infections.

Important times to wash hands include:

  • Before preparing or eating food.
  • After using the toilet.
  • After cleaning a child.
  • After coughing or sneezing when hands become contaminated.
  • After handling waste.
  • After caring for someone who is sick.

Hands should be washed thoroughly with soap and clean running water where available. When soap and water are not available, an appropriate alcohol-based hand sanitizer can be useful in many situations.

6. Oral Health

Oral health involves the health of the teeth, gums, mouth and surrounding structures.

Poor oral hygiene can contribute to tooth decay, gum disease, bad breath and discomfort. Good oral care includes regular tooth brushing, appropriate use of fluoride toothpaste, limiting excessive sugar intake and obtaining professional dental care when necessary.

7. Nutrition and a Balanced Diet

Nutrition is the process by which the body obtains and uses nutrients from food. Good nutrition provides energy and materials needed for growth, repair and normal body functions.

A balanced diet contains a variety of foods that provide the nutrients required by the body in appropriate amounts.

A healthy diet generally includes:

  • Fruits and vegetables.
  • Whole grains and other healthy sources of carbohydrates.
  • Protein sources.
  • Healthy fats.
  • Appropriate amounts of dairy or suitable alternatives where available.
  • Safe drinking water.

8. Major Nutrients

Nutrients are substances obtained from food that the body needs to function properly. They are commonly grouped into macronutrients and micronutrients.

Nutrient Main Function Examples of Sources
Carbohydrates Important source of energy. Grains, potatoes, fruits and other plant foods.
Proteins Support growth, repair and body structures. Beans, eggs, fish, meat and other protein-rich foods.
Fats Provide energy and support several body functions. Nuts, seeds, oils, fish and other foods.
Vitamins Support many body processes. Fruits, vegetables and other foods.
Minerals Support bones, blood, nerves and other functions. Vegetables, dairy foods, legumes and other foods.
Water Essential for many body processes and fluid balance. Drinking water and water-containing foods.

9. Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are an important source of energy for the body. They are found in many foods, including grains, roots, tubers, fruits and legumes.

The nutritional quality of carbohydrate sources matters. Foods containing whole grains and naturally occurring fibre can be useful parts of a healthy diet.

10. Proteins

Proteins are important for growth, maintenance and repair of body tissues. They are made from smaller substances called amino acids.

Protein can come from both animal and plant foods. Examples include eggs, fish, meat, milk, beans, peas, lentils, nuts and seeds.

11. Fats

Fats are an important source of energy and are involved in several normal body functions. They also help the body absorb certain vitamins.

A healthy diet should include appropriate sources of fat while avoiding excessive consumption of foods high in unhealthy fats.

12. Vitamins and Minerals

Vitamins and minerals are micronutrients because the body generally needs them in smaller quantities than carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

They support processes such as immunity, blood formation, bone health, nerve function and normal metabolism.

Important: Different vitamins and minerals perform different functions. A varied diet is generally the best way to obtain many essential micronutrients.

13. Water and Hydration

Water is essential for life. It helps regulate body temperature, transport substances, support digestion and maintain normal body functions.

Water requirements vary depending on factors such as age, activity, temperature, diet and health conditions. People who sweat heavily may require more fluids.

Safe drinking water is also important for preventing waterborne diseases.

14. Physical Activity and Exercise

Physical activity is movement of the body that uses energy. Exercise is a planned or structured form of physical activity.

Regular physical activity can improve cardiovascular fitness, muscle strength, physical function and general well-being. It can also support mental health and help reduce the risk of several chronic diseases.

Examples include:

  • Walking.
  • Running.
  • Cycling.
  • Swimming.
  • Sports.
  • Strength exercises.
  • Active household or work activities.

15. Rest and Sleep

Sleep is essential for physical and mental functioning. During sleep, the body and brain carry out important processes related to recovery, learning, memory and regulation.

Poor sleep can affect concentration, mood, learning, reaction time and general performance.

Healthy sleep habits include:

  • Keeping a consistent sleep schedule.
  • Creating a comfortable sleeping environment.
  • Limiting excessive screen use before bedtime.
  • Avoiding excessive caffeine, especially late in the day.
  • Allowing sufficient time for sleep.

16. Mental Health

Mental health involves emotional, psychological and social well-being. It influences how people think, feel, behave, make decisions and interact with others.

Everyone experiences changes in mood and stress. Experiencing a difficult period does not mean that a person has failed.

Mental health can be supported through healthy relationships, adequate rest, physical activity, social support and seeking professional help when necessary.

17. Emotional Health

Emotional health refers to the ability to recognize, understand and manage emotions in constructive ways.

Healthy emotional behaviour does not mean never feeling sadness, anger or fear. Instead, it involves responding to emotions in ways that do not unnecessarily harm oneself or others.

18. Stress

Stress is the physical and emotional response that can occur when a person faces demands or challenges.

Some stress can motivate people to prepare for examinations or meet responsibilities. However, persistent or overwhelming stress can negatively affect well-being.

Healthy ways to manage stress include:

  • Planning and organizing tasks.
  • Getting enough sleep.
  • Physical activity.
  • Talking to trusted people.
  • Taking appropriate breaks.
  • Using relaxation techniques.
  • Seeking professional support when needed.

19. Communicable Diseases

Communicable diseases are diseases that can spread from one person, animal or source to another through specific routes of transmission.

Different diseases spread in different ways. Transmission may occur through respiratory droplets or aerosols, contaminated food or water, blood, sexual contact, vectors or direct contact.

Prevention may include:

  • Hand hygiene.
  • Safe food and water.
  • Vaccination where recommended.
  • Vector control.
  • Appropriate sanitation.
  • Safe sexual practices.
  • Appropriate infection-control measures.

20. Non-Communicable Diseases

Non-communicable diseases are generally not spread directly from person to person. Many are chronic conditions influenced by multiple factors.

Examples include:

  • Diabetes.
  • Heart disease.
  • Stroke.
  • Some cancers.
  • Chronic respiratory diseases.

Risk can be influenced by factors such as tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, harmful alcohol use and some genetic and environmental factors.

21. Malaria

Malaria is an infectious disease caused by parasites transmitted through the bites of infected mosquitoes. It is particularly important in many tropical and subtropical regions.

Prevention strategies can include using appropriate mosquito control measures, reducing mosquito breeding sites and following local public-health recommendations.

Important: Fever or other symptoms that could represent malaria should be assessed by an appropriate healthcare professional, particularly in areas where malaria is common.

22. HIV and AIDS Awareness

HIV is a virus that attacks the body's immune system. Without effective treatment, HIV can eventually lead to AIDS, the most advanced stage of HIV infection.

HIV can be transmitted through certain body fluids, including blood, semen, vaginal fluids and breast milk, when appropriate transmission conditions are present.

Prevention and control include:

  • HIV testing and knowing one's status.
  • Using condoms correctly and consistently where appropriate.
  • Using sterile equipment for injections.
  • Accessing appropriate HIV prevention services.
  • Effective HIV treatment.
  • Prevention of mother-to-child transmission through appropriate care.

People living with HIV deserve respect and should not be discriminated against.

23. Sexually Transmitted Infections

Sexually transmitted infections, commonly called STIs, are infections that can be transmitted through sexual contact. Some can also have other routes of transmission.

Some STIs may have few or no symptoms, so testing can be important for people who may have been exposed.

Prevention can include:

  • Using condoms appropriately.
  • Reducing exposure to infection.
  • Testing when indicated.
  • Prompt treatment of diagnosed infections.
  • Following healthcare advice about partners.

24. Immunity and Vaccination

The immune system protects the body against many infectious agents and other threats.

Vaccines train the immune system to recognize specific infections and can significantly reduce the risk of disease and complications.

Vaccination schedules vary by age, country and individual circumstances, so people should follow recommendations from qualified health authorities.

25. Environmental Health

Environmental health examines how the physical environment affects human health.

Important environmental factors include clean water, sanitation, air quality, waste management, housing, food safety and exposure to hazardous substances.

26. Sanitation

Sanitation involves systems and practices that safely manage human waste and help maintain clean environments.

Poor sanitation can contribute to the spread of diseases, contamination of water sources and environmental pollution.

Good sanitation practices include:

  • Safe disposal of human waste.
  • Proper waste management.
  • Keeping toilets clean.
  • Protecting water sources.
  • Maintaining clean surroundings.

27. Food Safety

Food safety refers to practices that help prevent food from becoming contaminated and causing illness.

Basic food-safety practices include:

  • Washing hands before preparing food.
  • Keeping preparation surfaces clean.
  • Separating raw and cooked foods appropriately.
  • Cooking foods adequately.
  • Storing food safely.
  • Using safe water.

28. Substance Abuse

Substance misuse refers to harmful or inappropriate use of substances such as alcohol, tobacco, prescription medicines or other drugs.

Substance misuse can affect physical health, mental health, relationships, education, employment and financial stability.

Prevention includes education, supportive environments, early intervention and access to appropriate treatment and counselling.

29. Alcohol and Health

Alcohol can affect the brain, judgement, coordination and behaviour. Heavy or harmful alcohol use can contribute to accidents, injuries, dependence and several health problems.

People who drink should understand that alcohol consumption carries health risks, and some people should avoid alcohol completely, including during pregnancy and when taking certain medicines or when advised by a healthcare professional.

30. Tobacco and Smoking

Tobacco use is associated with serious health problems, including cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease and several cancers.

Second-hand smoke can also expose other people to harmful substances. Avoiding tobacco use and supporting people who want to quit are important public-health measures.

31. Sexual and Reproductive Health

Sexual and reproductive health involves physical, emotional and social well-being related to sexuality and reproductive systems.

Important areas include accurate health information, prevention of STIs, family planning, pregnancy care, consent, healthy relationships and access to appropriate healthcare.

Important: Sexual-health decisions should be based on accurate information, informed consent, respect and appropriate professional healthcare advice.

32. First Aid

First aid is the immediate assistance provided to a person who is injured or suddenly becomes ill before professional medical care is available.

The main purpose of first aid is to preserve life, prevent the condition from becoming worse and support recovery.

General first-aid principles

  1. Ensure the scene is reasonably safe.
  2. Assess the person's condition.
  3. Call or arrange for appropriate emergency help.
  4. Provide appropriate basic assistance within your training.
  5. Continue monitoring the person until help arrives.

33. Common First-Aid Situations

Minor cuts

Minor cuts should generally be protected from contamination and managed using appropriate basic first aid. Serious bleeding requires urgent medical assistance.

Burns

For many minor thermal burns, cooling the affected area with cool running water can help. Severe burns require urgent medical assessment.

Fainting

A person who faints should be protected from injury and monitored. Emergency help should be sought if the person does not recover promptly or if serious symptoms or injury are present.

Safety: First-aid procedures depend on the situation. Serious injuries, breathing problems, unconsciousness, severe bleeding or other emergencies require urgent professional medical assistance.

34. Maternal Health

Maternal health concerns the health of women during pregnancy, childbirth and the period after childbirth.

Antenatal care helps monitor pregnancy and identify possible complications. Skilled care during childbirth and appropriate postnatal care are important for the health of both mother and newborn.

35. Child Health

Children require adequate nutrition, vaccination, safe environments, hygiene, healthcare and supportive relationships for healthy growth and development.

Regular health monitoring can help identify developmental or health concerns early.

36. Health Education

Health education provides people with knowledge and skills that can help them make informed health decisions.

Effective health education should use accurate information and communicate in a way that people can understand and apply to their daily lives.

37. Disease Prevention

Disease prevention involves actions taken to reduce the risk of disease, detect problems early or reduce complications.

Level Meaning Example
Primary prevention Prevents disease before it occurs. Vaccination and healthy lifestyle practices.
Secondary prevention Detects disease early. Appropriate screening and early diagnosis.
Tertiary prevention Reduces complications and supports function after disease occurs. Rehabilitation and disease management.

38. Healthy Lifestyle

A healthy lifestyle consists of everyday choices that support physical, mental and social well-being.

  • Eat a varied and balanced diet.
  • Stay physically active.
  • Get adequate sleep.
  • Maintain good hygiene.
  • Avoid tobacco.
  • Avoid harmful substance use.
  • Maintain healthy relationships.
  • Attend appropriate health checks.
  • Seek medical help when necessary.

39. Health Services

Health services include the organizations, professionals and facilities that provide prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation and health education.

Examples include hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, laboratories, community health services and public-health programs.

People should seek appropriate professional care rather than relying only on information from unverified sources when they have serious or persistent health concerns.

40. Health and the Community

Individual health and community health are closely connected. Actions taken by one person can sometimes affect others.

Communities can promote health through clean environments, sanitation, health education, disease-prevention programs, vaccination services and access to healthcare.

41. Importance of Health Records

Health records contain information about a person's health history, examinations, diagnoses, treatments and other relevant information.

Accurate records help healthcare professionals understand a patient's history and make informed decisions.

Health information should be handled confidentially and accessed only by authorized people for legitimate purposes.

42. Common Health Problems Among Students

Students may experience health challenges related to academic pressure, poor sleep, inadequate nutrition, physical inactivity, stress or unhealthy habits.

Healthy student practices include:

  • Creating a realistic study timetable.
  • Getting adequate sleep.
  • Eating regular nutritious meals.
  • Drinking safe water.
  • Taking study breaks.
  • Staying physically active.
  • Seeking support when overwhelmed.

43. Health and Academic Performance

Health can strongly influence learning. Poor sleep, hunger, dehydration, stress and illness can make it difficult to concentrate and remember information.

Students can therefore improve their learning by treating health as part of their academic success rather than as something separate from education.

44. Health and Technology

Technology can provide useful health benefits such as access to educational information, communication with healthcare services and tools for tracking certain health behaviours.

However, health information found online should be evaluated carefully. Online information does not replace professional medical assessment when a person is seriously ill.

45. Importance of Prevention

Prevention is often an important part of maintaining health. Preventive actions can reduce exposure to disease and identify certain problems before they become more serious.

Prevention includes healthy behaviour, vaccination, hygiene, appropriate screening, environmental protection and health education.

46. Important Health Terms

Term Meaning
Health A state of physical, mental, emotional and social well-being.
Nutrition The process of obtaining and using nutrients from food.
Hygiene Practices that help maintain cleanliness and prevent disease.
Communicable disease A disease that can spread through specific routes of transmission.
Non-communicable disease A disease that generally does not spread directly between people.
Immunity The body's ability to defend against particular infections and other threats.
Sanitation Practices and systems for safe waste management and clean environments.

Course Summary

Health Principles teaches the knowledge and practices required to maintain and improve individual and community health. Health is broader than the absence of disease and includes physical, mental, emotional, social and other dimensions of well-being.

Good health is supported by balanced nutrition, physical activity, adequate sleep, personal hygiene, safe water, sanitation, disease prevention, healthy relationships and responsible lifestyle choices.

Health education helps individuals make informed decisions, while preventive measures can reduce the risk of disease and improve quality of life.

The main lesson is that maintaining good health is a continuous process involving personal choices, supportive communities, appropriate healthcare and healthy environments.
