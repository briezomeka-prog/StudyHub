BSCT 1111

Software Engineering Ethics

Expanded study notes covering professional ethics, software engineering responsibilities, privacy, security, intellectual property, artificial intelligence, ethical decision-making and professional conduct.

Introduction to Software Engineering Ethics

Software engineering is not only about writing computer programs, designing systems and solving technical problems. Software systems affect people's lives, businesses, governments, education, health, communication and many other areas of society.

Because software can have a major effect on people, software engineers must consider the ethical consequences of their decisions. A technically successful system may still be harmful if it violates privacy, discriminates against users, exposes sensitive information or is deliberately designed to deceive people.

Software engineering ethics therefore deals with the principles, values and professional responsibilities that guide software engineers when making decisions. It helps engineers determine not only what they can build, but also what they should build and how they should build it responsibly.

1. Meaning of Ethics

Ethics refers to the principles and values that help people distinguish between right and wrong behaviour. Ethics provides guidance for making responsible decisions, especially when a person faces a difficult situation where several choices are possible.

In software engineering, ethics helps professionals decide how they should behave toward users, employers, clients, colleagues and society.

Example: If a software engineer discovers that a company's application has a serious security weakness, the engineer should not simply ignore the problem. Ethical responsibility requires the problem to be reported and addressed appropriately.

2. Morality, Ethics and Law

Morality refers to personal or social beliefs about what is right and wrong. Ethics refers to organized principles used to guide behaviour. Law consists of rules established and enforced by governments.

Concept Meaning Example
Morality Personal or social beliefs about right and wrong. Believing that stealing is wrong.
Ethics Principles used to guide responsible behaviour. Protecting confidential client information.
Law Official rules established by authorities. Following copyright and data-protection laws.

Something can be legal but still raise ethical concerns. Therefore, software engineers should not assume that everything permitted by law is automatically ethically appropriate.

3. Professional Ethics

Professional ethics refers to the standards of behaviour expected from people working in a particular profession. It establishes responsibilities that professionals should follow while performing their duties.

Software engineers are expected to demonstrate honesty, responsibility, competence, respect and integrity. They should provide accurate information and avoid deliberately misleading clients or users.

Important professional values

  • Honesty
  • Integrity
  • Competence
  • Responsibility
  • Confidentiality
  • Respect for others
  • Accountability

4. Software Engineering as a Profession

Software engineering is a professional discipline concerned with the systematic development, operation, maintenance and improvement of software systems.

A software engineer has responsibilities beyond producing code. The engineer must understand requirements, consider risks, protect users, test software properly and communicate honestly with stakeholders.

Professional software engineering therefore combines technical knowledge with professional judgement and ethical responsibility.

5. Why Ethics Matters in Software Engineering

Ethics is important because software systems can affect thousands or even millions of people. Poor ethical decisions can result in financial loss, privacy violations, discrimination, security breaches or physical harm.

Reasons ethics is important

  • Protects users from harm.
  • Protects personal and confidential information.
  • Builds trust between developers and users.
  • Encourages responsible innovation.
  • Reduces misuse of technology.
  • Improves professional reputation.
  • Supports compliance with laws and regulations.

6. ACM and IEEE Code of Ethics

Professional organizations such as the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) and the IEEE Computer Society have developed professional codes of ethics to guide computing professionals.

These codes encourage professionals to act in the public interest, avoid harm, maintain competence, respect privacy and confidentiality, and behave honestly.

A code of ethics does not remove the need for personal judgement. Instead, it provides a framework that professionals can use when dealing with difficult situations.

7. Public Interest

One of the most important responsibilities of a software engineer is to consider the welfare of the public. Software should be developed with reasonable attention to safety, security, reliability and potential harm.

Example: Imagine engineers developing software that controls medical equipment. They must consider patient safety rather than focusing only on completing the project quickly.

8. Responsibility to Clients and Employers

Software engineers have responsibilities toward the organizations and clients who employ them. Engineers should perform their work competently, communicate important risks and protect confidential business information.

However, loyalty to a client or employer does not mean blindly following every instruction. If an instruction could seriously harm users or violate professional responsibilities, the engineer should raise the concern through appropriate channels.

9. Product Quality

Software engineers should aim to produce systems that are reliable, secure, maintainable and suitable for their intended purpose.

Quality does not simply mean that software looks attractive. A high-quality system should perform correctly, handle expected situations and provide reasonable protection against errors and security threats.

Characteristics of quality software

  • Correctness
  • Reliability
  • Security
  • Usability
  • Maintainability
  • Performance
  • Accessibility

10. Professional Judgment and Integrity

Professional judgement means using knowledge, experience and careful reasoning to make responsible decisions.

Integrity means being honest and consistent in professional conduct. A software engineer with integrity should not deliberately hide serious defects, falsify test results or claim credit for another person's work.

Example: If testing reveals that an application frequently crashes, an engineer should report the results honestly instead of changing the testing report to make the software appear successful.

11. Management and Leadership Ethics

Software managers and team leaders also have ethical responsibilities. They should create an environment where employees can report problems without unreasonable fear.

Managers should avoid unrealistic demands that encourage employees to ignore testing, security or quality simply to meet deadlines.

Ethical leadership involves:

  • Fair treatment of employees.
  • Clear communication.
  • Respect for professional opinions.
  • Responsible project planning.
  • Supporting quality and security.
  • Taking responsibility for important decisions.

12. Privacy

Privacy is the right of individuals to have appropriate control over information about themselves. Software systems frequently collect personal information such as names, addresses, contact details, location information and online activity.

Engineers should consider why information is being collected, how it will be used, who can access it and how long it should be kept.

Important: Collecting large amounts of personal information simply because technology makes it possible does not automatically make the collection ethically acceptable.

13. Confidentiality

Confidentiality means protecting information that should not be disclosed to unauthorized people.

Software engineers may have access to company source code, passwords, customer information, financial records or business strategies. Such information should be handled responsibly.

Ways of protecting confidentiality

  • Use strong authentication.
  • Limit access to authorized people.
  • Use appropriate encryption.
  • Avoid sharing passwords.
  • Follow organizational security policies.
  • Secure stored and transmitted information.

14. Intellectual Property

Intellectual property refers to creations of the human mind that may receive legal protection. In computing, intellectual property can include software, source code, documentation, designs, logos, algorithms and other creative works.

Software engineers must respect the ownership rights of creators and organizations. Copying or distributing protected software without permission can create both legal and ethical problems.

15. Copyright

Copyright protects original creative works. Software code, documentation, graphics and other digital materials may be protected by copyright depending on the applicable law.

Developers should understand the licenses associated with code and resources they use. They should not assume that anything available online is automatically free to copy.

16. Patents

A patent is a legal protection that can grant rights over certain inventions or technical innovations for a specified period, subject to applicable law.

Software-related patent rules vary between jurisdictions. Software engineers should therefore understand the relevant laws and organizational policies when working with potentially patented technologies.

17. Software Licensing

A software license describes the conditions under which software may be used, copied, modified or distributed.

Developers should read and understand licenses before using third-party libraries or software components.

License Type General Idea
Proprietary Controlled by an owner with specific usage restrictions.
Open Source Source code is made available under specified license terms.
Freeware Software may be available without a purchase price, but terms still apply.

18. Open Source Ethics

Open-source software allows people to inspect, use, modify and distribute source code according to the particular license.

Ethical use of open-source software requires respecting the license. Developers should provide required notices, preserve required copyright information and follow redistribution conditions.

19. Security and Responsible Development

Software security is an ethical responsibility because insecure systems can expose users to serious harm.

Security should be considered throughout the software development lifecycle rather than added only after the system has been completed.

Important security practices

  • Secure authentication.
  • Authorization and access control.
  • Input validation.
  • Secure storage of sensitive information.
  • Regular security testing.
  • Software updates and patching.
  • Secure error handling.

20. Artificial Intelligence Ethics

Artificial intelligence can provide significant benefits, but it can also create ethical challenges. AI systems may influence employment, education, finance, healthcare, security and many other areas.

Engineers developing AI systems should consider whether the system can cause unfair discrimination, produce harmful decisions, misuse personal information or create misleading results.

Important AI ethical concerns

  • Bias and discrimination
  • Privacy
  • Transparency
  • Accountability
  • Safety
  • Human oversight
  • Misuse

21. Bias and Fairness

Bias occurs when a system produces systematically unfair outcomes for certain individuals or groups. Bias can enter a system through data, design decisions, assumptions or the way an algorithm is developed and evaluated.

Engineers should test systems carefully and consider whether their designs could disadvantage particular groups.

Example: If an automated application system consistently rejects qualified applicants from a particular group because of biased historical training data, engineers should investigate and address the problem.

22. Transparency and Explainability

Transparency means providing appropriate information about how a system works, what information it uses and what its limitations are.

Explainability is especially important when automated systems make decisions that significantly affect people.

Users should not always be expected to accept an automated decision simply because a computer produced it.

23. Accountability

Accountability means accepting responsibility for decisions and actions. In software engineering, responsibility can involve developers, managers, organizations and other stakeholders.

When a system causes a problem, organizations should investigate what happened, correct the problem and take reasonable measures to prevent similar incidents.

24. Data Ethics and Big Data

Big data refers to very large and complex collections of data that can be analyzed to identify patterns and support decisions.

Data ethics considers whether data is collected, stored, analyzed and shared responsibly.

Data ethics questions

  • Was the data collected appropriately?
  • Do people understand how their data will be used?
  • Is unnecessary personal information being collected?
  • Who can access the information?
  • How securely is the information stored?
  • Could analysis produce unfair outcomes?

25. Cybercrime and Misuse of Technology

Technology can be misused for activities such as unauthorized access, fraud, identity theft, malware distribution and other harmful activities.

Software professionals should use their technical skills responsibly. Having the ability to access or manipulate a system does not give a person ethical permission to do so.

26. Whistleblowing

Whistleblowing occurs when an individual reports serious wrongdoing within an organization to an appropriate authority or, in some circumstances, externally.

A software engineer may face a difficult situation if an employer ignores a serious safety, security or legal problem.

Ethical whistleblowing should involve careful consideration, documentation and use of appropriate reporting channels whenever possible.

27. Workplace Ethics

Ethical behaviour in the workplace involves treating colleagues fairly and respectfully.

Examples of good workplace ethics

  • Respecting colleagues.
  • Giving proper credit for work.
  • Avoiding harassment and discrimination.
  • Communicating honestly.
  • Keeping professional commitments.
  • Protecting confidential information.
  • Accepting constructive criticism.

28. Conflict of Interest

A conflict of interest occurs when personal interests could influence professional judgement or create the appearance of inappropriate influence.

Example: A software engineer responsible for selecting a supplier may have a conflict of interest if the supplier is owned by a close relative.

Conflicts should be disclosed and managed appropriately.

29. Ethical Decision-Making

Software engineers may face situations where there is no simple answer. An ethical decision-making process can help them analyze the situation carefully.

Steps in ethical decision-making

  1. Identify the ethical problem.
  2. Collect relevant facts.
  3. Identify affected stakeholders.
  4. Identify possible choices.
  5. Consider the benefits and harms of each choice.
  6. Check professional codes and applicable laws.
  7. Choose the most responsible option.
  8. Document and communicate the decision where appropriate.
  9. Review the outcome.

30. Stakeholders in Software Engineering

Stakeholders are people or organizations affected by a software project or who have an interest in its outcome.

Stakeholder Possible Interest
Users Safety, privacy, usability and reliability.
Client Meeting requirements and achieving business objectives.
Developers Professional standards, working conditions and technical quality.
Managers Budget, schedule, quality and organizational goals.
Government Compliance with applicable laws and public welfare.
Society Safety, fairness and responsible technological development.

31. Ethical Case Study: Privacy

Suppose a company develops a mobile application that collects users' location information. The developers discover that the application collects location data even when the feature is not necessary for the service.

Ethical questions

  • Do users know that their location is being collected?
  • Is the collection necessary?
  • Who can access the data?
  • How long is the data stored?
  • Could the data be misused?

The ethical approach is to minimize unnecessary collection, provide appropriate information to users and protect collected information.

32. Ethical Case Study: Software Quality

Imagine a development team discovers a serious software defect one week before a product launch. Management wants the software released immediately because delaying the launch would be expensive.

The engineers must consider the possible consequences. If the defect could cause serious harm, hiding it simply to meet a deadline would be ethically irresponsible.

The team should communicate the risk clearly and recommend an appropriate solution based on the seriousness of the defect.

33. Ethical Case Study: Artificial Intelligence

Consider an organization using an AI system to help select job applicants. After deployment, analysis shows that qualified applicants from a particular group are being rejected at a much higher rate.

Engineers should investigate the data, model and decision process. They should determine whether the system contains a significant source of unfair bias and recommend appropriate corrective measures.

The key lesson is that automation does not automatically make a decision fair or objective.

34. Common Ethical Challenges in Software Engineering

Software engineers can encounter ethical challenges throughout the development process.

  • Pressure to meet unrealistic deadlines.
  • Hiding software defects.
  • Unauthorized use of software.
  • Misuse of customer information.
  • Security vulnerabilities.
  • Discrimination in automated systems.
  • Conflicts of interest.
  • Plagiarism and intellectual-property violations.
  • Misleading advertising about software capabilities.
  • Failure to report serious risks.

35. Professional Responsibilities of Software Engineers

A professional software engineer should understand that technical ability creates responsibility. The engineer should use knowledge carefully and consider the effect of software on users and society.

Major responsibilities include:

  • Developing reliable software.
  • Protecting user information.
  • Maintaining professional competence.
  • Being honest about limitations.
  • Respecting intellectual property.
  • Reporting serious risks.
  • Following applicable laws and professional standards.
  • Considering the public interest.

36. Maintaining Professional Competence

Technology changes rapidly. A software engineer who stops learning may eventually lack the knowledge required to develop safe and reliable systems.

Professional competence therefore requires continuous learning, training, research and practical experience.

Ways to maintain competence

  • Reading technical documentation.
  • Attending training and workshops.
  • Practicing programming.
  • Studying new technologies.
  • Learning from professional communities.
  • Reviewing previous mistakes and projects.

37. Ethics Throughout the Software Development Life Cycle

Ethics should be considered during every stage of software development.

SDLC Stage Ethical Consideration
Requirements Understand user needs and identify potential harm.
Design Consider privacy, security, accessibility and fairness.
Development Write responsible and maintainable code.
Testing Report results honestly and investigate serious defects.
Deployment Ensure appropriate security and user communication.
Maintenance Fix vulnerabilities and respond responsibly to incidents.

38. Ethics and Accessibility

Accessibility means designing technology so that people with different abilities can use it effectively.

Ethical software design should consider users who may have visual, hearing, physical or cognitive limitations.

Examples of accessibility features

  • Alternative text for meaningful images.
  • Keyboard navigation.
  • Readable text.
  • Clear interface design.
  • Captions for appropriate multimedia.
  • Good colour contrast.

39. Ethics and Environmental Responsibility

Software may indirectly affect the environment through energy consumption, hardware requirements and data-centre operations.

Engineers can contribute to responsible technology by considering efficient software design, resource usage and the useful lifetime of systems.

40. Ethics and Social Media Software

Social media systems can influence communication, information sharing and public opinion. Engineers working on such systems need to consider issues such as privacy, misinformation, harassment, recommendation systems and user safety.

Ethical design does not necessarily mean preventing all harmful content through technology alone. It involves identifying risks and building reasonable safeguards while respecting legitimate use.

41. Ethics and Misinformation

Software platforms can contribute to the rapid spread of false or misleading information. Engineers should consider how platform design, recommendation algorithms and automated systems may amplify harmful content.

Responsible engineering involves evaluating foreseeable risks, improving system transparency and supporting appropriate mechanisms for reporting and addressing harmful information.

42. Four Important Ethical Principles

Several broad principles can help guide responsible software engineering decisions.

  1. Respect for people: Users and colleagues should be treated with dignity and fairness.
  2. Do no unnecessary harm: Engineers should identify and reduce foreseeable risks.
  3. Honesty: Professionals should provide truthful information about systems, risks and capabilities.
  4. Responsibility: Engineers should accept responsibility for their professional decisions and take reasonable action when problems are identified.

43. How to Handle an Ethical Problem

When a software engineer encounters an ethical problem, reacting emotionally or ignoring the issue may make the situation worse. A structured approach is more useful.

Recommended approach

  1. Clearly describe the problem.
  2. Identify everyone affected.
  3. Gather reliable facts.
  4. Identify professional and legal requirements.
  5. List possible actions.
  6. Consider benefits, risks and consequences.
  7. Seek appropriate professional advice.
  8. Select the most responsible option.
  9. Document important decisions.
  10. Review the result.

44. Benefits of Ethical Software Engineering

  • Improves public trust.
  • Protects users.
  • Improves software quality.
  • Reduces security and privacy risks.
  • Protects professional reputation.
  • Encourages responsible innovation.
  • Supports long-term organizational success.
  • Reduces the likelihood of harmful consequences.

45. Consequences of Unethical Software Engineering

Unethical software engineering can produce serious consequences. These consequences may affect individuals, organizations and society.

  • Loss of user trust.
  • Financial losses.
  • Privacy violations.
  • Security breaches.
  • Legal consequences.
  • Damage to professional reputation.
  • Discrimination.
  • Physical or social harm in high-risk systems.

Course Summary

Software Engineering Ethics provides principles that guide responsible professional behaviour in software development. Software engineers are responsible not only for producing working software but also for considering how their systems affect users, organizations and society.

Major ethical areas include privacy, confidentiality, security, intellectual property, professional integrity, accountability, fairness, artificial intelligence and responsible decision-making.

Ethical software engineering requires professionals to identify potential harm, communicate honestly, respect the rights of others, maintain professional competence and make decisions that consider both technical requirements and human consequences.

The central lesson is that being able to build something does not automatically mean that it is responsible to build or use it in every possible way.
